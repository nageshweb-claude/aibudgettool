How the EMI is worked out

EMI means equated monthly instalment: the same payment every month until the loan is cleared. Each payment covers that month's interest first, and the rest reduces what you owe.

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)
  1. P is the amount you borrow.
  2. r is the monthly interest rate: the yearly rate divided by 12, then by 100. So 9% a year becomes 0.0075.
  3. n is the number of monthly payments. A 20-year loan has 240.

In the early years most of each payment goes to interest. Open the yearly schedule above to see the split change over time.

Example

You borrow ₹10 lakh at 9% a year for 5 years (60 months).

Monthly rate r = 9 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0075.

EMI = ₹20,758 a month.

Over 60 months you pay ₹12,45,501 in total, so the interest is ₹2,45,501.

Three ways to pay less interest

Choose the shortest tenure you can afford. At 8.5%, a ₹50 lakh loan costs about ₹54 lakh in interest over 20 years, but about ₹39 lakh over 15 years.

Prepay early. Extra payments in the first few years cut the principal while interest is still the biggest part of each EMI.

Compare rates, not just EMIs. A longer tenure can make a costly loan look cheap. Look at total interest before you sign.

Questions people ask

What is EMI?

EMI stands for equated monthly instalment. It is the fixed amount you pay your lender every month until a loan is fully repaid. Each EMI pays that month's interest and part of the principal.

How do I calculate EMI?

Use EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. The calculator above does this instantly.

Does this work for home, car and personal loans?

Yes. All standard reducing-balance loans use the same formula. Use the quick-start buttons to load typical home, car, personal and education loan settings, then change them to match your offer.

Why is so much of my early EMI interest?

Interest is charged on the balance you still owe. At the start the balance is highest, so the interest part is biggest. As the balance falls, more of each EMI goes to the principal.

Is a longer tenure better?

A longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI but raises the total interest you pay, often by a lot. Pick the shortest tenure whose EMI fits comfortably in your budget.

Does the calculator include processing fees or insurance?

No. It shows the EMI on the loan amount only. Add any fee or insurance your lender includes in the loan to the loan amount to see its effect.

Can I use currencies other than rupees?

Yes. Pick any of 22 currencies from the menu next to the loan amount. The maths is the same; only the symbol and number grouping change.
