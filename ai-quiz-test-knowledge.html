Test Your AI Knowledge: Free Quiz

Published 19 June 2026 · 2 min read

🎮 Ready to play? Jump straight to the quiz →

Why Test Your AI Knowledge?

AI is moving fast. In March 2026 alone, over 12 major model releases dropped in a single week. New terms like "agentic AI," "MCP," and "constitutional AI" are entering everyday conversations. Whether you're a student, professional, developer, or just curious — knowing what these tools can do gives you a real advantage.

Our free interactive quiz doesn't just test you — it teaches you. Every question comes with a detailed explanation, so you learn something whether you get it right or wrong.

What's Inside the Quiz

5 categories, 20 questions each:

🤖 OpenAI — GPT models, ChatGPT features, DALL·E, Sora, Whisper, pricing, and architecture. Do you know how the GPT-6 tiers differ, or what Codex does?

💎 Google Gemini — Gemini Pro, Flash, Nano, AI Studio, Workspace integration, TPUs, and Google's AI ecosystem. What makes Gemini natively multimodal?

🧠 Claude AI — Anthropic, Constitutional AI, Claude Code, MCP, Artifacts, and the full Claude model lineup. Why does Claude lead SWE-bench?

⚡ Generative AI — Foundational concepts: Transformers, tokens, temperature, embeddings, RAG, RLHF, fine-tuning. The vocabulary you need to understand any AI conversation.

🚀 Agentic AI — The frontier: AI agents, tool use, multi-agent systems, orchestrators, guardrails, computer use, and autonomous workflows.

How Scoring Works

Each round picks 10 random questions from your chosen category, so the quiz is different every time. Every correct answer earns 10 points (100 max per round). At the end, you're graded: Beginner (0-30), Intermediate (40-50), Advanced (60-70), or Expert (80-100). Get 80+ and confetti rains down.

Your scores are saved to the leaderboard — compete with yourself across categories, or share with friends to see who knows AI better.

After the Quiz: Flashcard Review

The real learning happens in the review. After your results, click "Review Answers" to see flashcards of every question — your answer, the correct answer, and a detailed explanation. This spaced repetition approach helps concepts stick in long-term memory.
