How transfer time is calculated

Time (seconds) = file size in bits ÷ speed in bits per second
  1. Convert the file size to bits. 1 byte is 8 bits, and 1 GB is 1,000,000,000 bytes.
  2. Convert the speed to bits per second. 100 Mbps is 100,000,000 bits per second.
  3. Divide the first number by the second.

Real transfers are slower than the plan speed because of Wi-Fi signal, network protocol overhead and the other end's server. The real-world line assumes you get about 85% of the advertised speed.

Example

Downloading a 4 GB file on a 100 Mbps connection.

4 GB = 4 × 1,000,000,000 × 8 = 32,000,000,000 bits.

32,000,000,000 ÷ 100,000,000 = 320 seconds = 5 min 20 s.

At 85% efficiency, plan for about 6 min 16 s.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?

Mbps is megabits per second, and MB/s is megabytes per second. There are 8 bits in a byte, so 100 Mbps equals 12.5 MB/s. Internet plans use Mbps, while browsers and download apps usually show MB/s.

How long does it take to download 1 GB?

At 100 Mbps, 1 GB takes 80 seconds at full speed. At 25 Mbps it takes about 5 minutes 20 seconds, and at 1 Gbps about 8 seconds.

Why is my download slower than the calculator says?

Plan speeds are a maximum. Wi-Fi distance, other devices on the network, the website's server and protocol overhead all reduce real speed. The real-world figure shows a more realistic estimate.

Does this use 1000 or 1024 for KB, MB and GB?

It uses 1000 (decimal units), the same as disk makers and internet providers. Some operating systems show sizes in 1024-based units, which makes a file look a few percent smaller. Use the unit converter to switch between GB and GiB.

Does it work for uploads?

Yes. Enter your upload speed instead. Many home plans have much slower upload than download speeds.
